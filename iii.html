Part 2 · Height is pitch

A scale is a modular type scale

A modular type scale sets fixed steps between sizes: body, subhead, heading. A musical scale does the same with pitch. The Alphabeat uses eight steps, and like doubling 24 pt to 48 pt, the last step is exactly twice the first. Musicians call that doubling an octave. Tap the steps.

The letters climb this ladder in alphabet order: a is the first step, b the second, and so on. Tall letters jump to the next ladder up, twice as high, the way an ascender reaches above the x-height. Letters with a tail drop to the ladder below.

Part 3 · Shape is sound colour

Round sans, monoline, sharp display

Set the same word in a rounded sans and in a sharp, angular display face: same letters, different feeling. In music this is called timbre, the colour of a sound. All three below play the same note, so you only hear the shape.

The bars show the overtones, the hidden extra pitches inside every sound. Think of them as the sound's detailing, like serifs, ink traps or sharp corners. Smooth curves have almost none, so they sound soft. Every corner adds more, so the sound gets brighter and buzzier.

Part 4 · Mirroring

b d p q: the same glyph, the same melody

Type designers often draw b once, then mirror it into d, flip it into p, and rotate it into q. Composers like Bach do exactly this with melodies. Here b plays a short three-note melody (a motif), and the other three letters transform it the same way their shapes are transformed.

Part 5 · Monograms

Names that are melodies

A monogram turns initials into a mark. Composers did the same with sound. In German, the notes are named A to H, and S is read as Es (E flat). So some names can be played directly. Switch on "Only letters that are note names" in the machine, or tap a name.

Part 6 · The shapes in detail

Every letter part, and what it sounds like

All letters sit on the same guide lines: ascender 750, x-height 500, centre 250, baseline 0, descender −250 (on a 1000-unit em). The wave always starts and ends on the centre line. Each part of a letter is drawn with one fixed wave shape, read left to right in writing order, top before bottom.

Part 7 · Letter by letter

How to read each letter

Each card shows the real letter next to its wave on the same guide lines, then walks through the letter part by part: which shape it becomes, and what you hear. Press play to hear it.

Numbers

Numbers are oldstyle figures, the kind that sit among lowercase: 0 1 2 at x-height, 6 8 rising, 3 4 5 7 9 dropping. All share one width (tabular), so they all play the same length. Each digit is also a step on the ladder in Part 2.

Part 8 · Where this comes from

Drawing sound before The Alphabeat

1950s–70s

Animator Norman McLaren drew sound straight onto film, as in Synchromy (1971).

1960s

Daphne Oram built Oramics, a machine that played shapes painted on film strips.

1977

Composer Iannis Xenakis created UPIC: draw a line on a tablet and hear it.

Now

The Alphabeat: every letter is a drawn wave, so writing becomes composing.
